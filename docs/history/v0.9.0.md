> Upgrade with `docker compose up -d --pull always`; update `KIKOTO_IMAGE`
> first if it is pinned.

Kikoto v0.9.0 makes **Manage lyrics** a lyrics workshop: lyrics per language,
speech-to-lyrics transcription, and LLM translation of lyrics and metadata,
all reviewed before saving and governed by one content safety policy, with
**Kikoto Helper** for local models. It also adds **Subscriptions** for new
releases, smart lists in Favorites, official bundle contents, and **Edit
files**, and makes circle and voice actor pages much faster.

<details>
<summary><strong>Lyrics, transcription, and translation</strong></summary>

- **Manage lyrics** keeps one lyrics file per track and language. A language
  strip switches between **Fallback**, the lyrics used when none of a
  listener's languages has lyrics, and Japanese, Simplified Chinese,
  Traditional Chinese, English, and Korean. Lyrics assigned before the upgrade
  become fallback lyrics.
- **Manage lyrics** puts its sections in a tab bar under the title, with the
  language strip, folder alignment, and reset in one toolbar. Each folder of
  tracks counts the tracks that have lyrics, a mark on every row shows whether
  the track has lyrics, and the lyrics preview lists line times in their own
  column.
- **Lyrics files** replaces **From remote**. Like **Edit files**, it edits in
  three columns the lyrics Kikoto manages in one `lyrics_kikoto` folder inside
  the work folder, with a folder per language such as
  `lyrics_kikoto/zh-hans`. A lyrics folder the work ships, such as `lyrics`,
  stays one of the work's files and is never filled or emptied as managed
  lyrics. **Lyrics files** copies or moves lyrics of the work folder there
  (administrators set the starting choice under **Lyrics files** in Settings →
  Library), copies lyrics from the work's other local folders and editions,
  downloads them from remote editions, and deletes them. You choose the
  language; Kikoto does not guess it. A different file with the same name is
  resolved in the preview by keeping, replacing, keeping both, or renaming,
  and added lyrics can be assigned to the tracks they match.
- **Manage files** has **Manage as lyrics**, which opens **Lyrics files** with
  the selected lyrics files, and hides the lyrics in those folders unless
  **Show in the list** is on.
- **Transcribe** lists the local tracks with the languages they have lyrics
  in, filtered to the tracks without lyrics or without lyrics in the original
  language, and turns the speech of the selected tracks into timed lyrics with
  a configured speech recognition service. The audio language starts as the
  work's original language. Each transcription is an Activity workflow that
  sends parts of at most five minutes, cut in quiet moments, and gives the
  queue to other waiting work between parts. Service configuration,
  permissions, and content policy are checked again right before audio is
  sent. **Add to lyrics** saves the reviewed result as a WebVTT file in the
  language's lyrics folder and assigns it to the track.
- **Translate** shows the local tracks against the lyrics languages, green
  where a track has lyrics and red where it has none. Choosing red cells, or a
  whole language column, translates those tracks with a configured LLM
  provider from their original-language lyrics, or from another language
  chosen in the row. Each translation is an Activity workflow that keeps the
  file's timing and waits for review; **Add to lyrics** saves it in the target
  language's lyrics folder under the track's path and assigns it to the track.
- **Check rating** in **Transcribe** and **Translate** shows the content
  safety class of the work or of each lyrics file without sending anything,
  and marks the services that may not receive it. The check runs only when
  pressed.
- **Edit files**, **Lyrics files**, and **Add to lyrics** never replace a
  folder or link at a target. A file that sits where a folder belongs is named
  and can only be kept or have the incoming file renamed, and a file or folder
  Kikoto did not create that blocks the lyrics folder asks you to rename or
  move it.
- A failed lyrics edit restores moved source files even when an earlier
  publication occupies their original path. If restoration is obstructed,
  staged files and their recovery mapping remain on the target storage pool.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Subscriptions</strong></summary>

- A new **Subscriptions** page collects new works from the circles, series,
  and voice actors you subscribe to with the bell beside **Favorite** on their
  pages. Subscribing is separate from Favorite. A subscription starts from the
  catalog as it is, so existing works never flood the feed; a catalog that was
  not pulled recently is pulled first, now or at the next refresh.
- **New releases** groups one card per work family by the day it reached your
  feed, as a new release, a new language edition, or a work that Availability
  Watch found available. Cards follow the Library card modes, and a page holds
  5, 10, or 15 days that have works. The navigation counts entries added since
  you last opened it, and a refresh that adds any sends one notification
  instead of one per work.
- A circle subscription includes its series: a series of a subscribed circle
  cannot be subscribed separately, and subscribing to the circle replaces its
  series subscriptions after a confirmation.
- One shared **Refresh schedule** (every 24 hours by default) pulls each
  subscribed catalog once, syncs the metadata of new works, and then checks
  Availability Watch. A subscription can also add its new works to
  Availability Watch.
- Upgrading turns each enabled Follow a circle, series, or voice actor
  trigger into subscriptions of the account that configured it and deletes the
  trigger; tag templates, release ranges, and work limits are not kept. An
  already pulled catalog becomes the baseline, so its works are not reported
  as new. Availability Watch takes the refresh interval it had (24 hours when
  it had none) and is checked after each refresh unless its schedule was off.
- Availability Watch's configuration and pool move to the Subscriptions page;
  Workflows keeps its run history and links there.
- The Follow presets are renamed **Sync a circle catalog**, **Sync a series
  catalog**, and **Sync a voice actor catalog**, under a **Catalog sync**
  category, and run on demand only. Their triggers section links to
  Subscriptions, and the circle and voice actor **More** menus read **Sync this
  circle's catalog…** and **Sync this voice actor's catalog…**.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Favorites and lists</strong></summary>

- Favorites has smart lists. A smart list shows the works that carry all of
  its required tags, at least one of its any-of tags, and none of its excluded
  tags, using shared metadata tags and your own tags. Matching works are not
  favorites; works you add by hand are, and a matching work you remove stays
  out. The shelf counts and marks works that started to match since you last
  chose **Mark all as seen**, and **Convert to regular list** keeps the
  current works as an ordinary list. Personal data exports leave smart lists
  out.
- **Add to list** on work cards and work detail has a search field, saves each
  list you check or clear at once, and creates a list from a name no list has.
  It notes a smart list that holds the work through its rule. **Change lists**
  on selected Favorites works shows what each list will add or remove before
  you apply it and can also create a list. On phones both open as a bottom
  sheet.
- **Add list** and a shelf's **Edit list** open a focused editor for that one
  list, with the icon beside the name, a note when another list has the same
  name, and **Delete list**. **Edit lists** lists every list on its own and
  returns to it after an edit. Moving a list saves at once without a
  notification or switching shelves. Japanese, Korean, and Chinese call
  **Edit lists** "Manage lists" so it differs from a shelf's Edit list.
- The List button on a work's detail page names the list that holds the work.
  When several do, it names the first and counts the rest, and its tooltip
  names them all.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Library and work detail</strong></summary>

- Official bundles such as complete packs list the works they contain.
  Metadata sync fills the list when DLsite names the contents, and a library
  editor adds or removes works under **Bundle contents** in the metadata
  editor, with suggestions from the bundle's DLsite series. The bundle's
  detail shows **Includes** and each work's detail shows **Included in**; the
  works stay separate. Searching for a work's code or title also finds the
  bundles that include it.
- **Manage files** has **Edit files**, a three-column edit of one local
  folder: the work's local folders and remote editions on the left, the files
  of the chosen place in the middle, and the final folder on the right. It
  copies files from the work's other local folders, downloads files from
  remote editions, and removes the files cleared in the folder, as one
  Activity workflow. **Check sources** asks every remote source about each
  edition of the work and refreshes the remote editions it lists. **Fetch** on
  the detail page of a work that has a local folder opens it.
- A work whose files lie in several local folders shows each folder in its
  directory, lists one folder at a time in **Manage files**, and tells a
  library manager how to bring the copies into one. The same notice appears
  when another edition of the work is in the library.
- **Quick select** in **Manage files** has **Find identical files**, which
  compares the local files of every edition and folder of the work by content,
  marks the files that have an identical copy, and can select the copies that
  also exist in another folder or are repeated in the listed one.
- **Manage files** keeps the folder choice, **Quick select**, and **Edit
  files** in one toolbar above the list. **Quick select** holds the selections
  by location and format, the head of the list selects the whole folder, and a
  file names its copies only when it has more than one.
- **Cards** leads the display options of the Library, circles, voice actors,
  and Subscriptions. Automatic wide-screen columns fit up to 5 full, 6
  compact, or 7 mini cards in a row and fewer in a narrower window. Choosing a
  card mode returns wide-screen columns to automatic and sets works per page
  and Library cover source marks to that mode's starting point: 24 works for
  **Full** and **Compact**, and 48 works with no source marks for **Mini**.
- The Library and Favorites offer 24, 48, or 96 works per page on every tab.
  Remote sources drop the 12-per-page choice, and a device that had chosen it
  shows 24.
- The bottom page bar shows more page numbers on wider screens and keeps the
  same number of items on every page, so its arrows stay in place. The
  ellipsis between page numbers opens a field that goes to a typed page.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Circles and voice actors</strong></summary>

- The voice actor list, the circle list, and circle pages load much faster,
  and a circle page stays as fast however large the rest of the library is.
  The favorite circles and voice actors on Favorites only summarize the
  creators you favorited, so these lists hold up other pages far less when
  many people use the server at once.
- A voice actor's works are paged on the server. The search, the availability
  filter, and the chosen order apply across all of the voice actor's works
  before a page is returned, and selection covers the page on screen. The
  search matches like a plain Library search, including titles in other
  languages and voice actor names, and every edition of a work counts as one
  work.
- **Change cover…** in the **More** menu of a circle or voice actor's detail
  page chooses one of their works as their cover, on the tile and the detail
  summary. **Latest work** in the same dialog restores the cover that follows
  each new release. Each account keeps its own choice.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Metadata and scans</strong></summary>

- **Translate with LLM** in the metadata editor translates a work's title,
  description, and missing shared tag names into a language the chosen model
  supports. **Check content without sending** shows how the content safety
  policy classifies the request without contacting the provider. The result
  is a preview that waits for review; accepted titles and descriptions are
  marked **LLM** in the language menu and on cards, are searchable and
  sortable, and never override a manual title or an official edition. A
  changed original field retires its translation, and **Revoke** and
  **Restore** take a translation out of presentation or bring it back.
- **Queue background translation** translates up to 100 named works as an
  Activity task that survives restarts and keeps finished parts. A request
  whose result is uncertain is never sent again automatically; **Resend
  uncertain request** asks you to acknowledge that another charge may occur.
- A local scan keeps every folder in which it finds a work's code as a folder
  of that work and indexes all of them, instead of raising duplicate folders
  for review in Activity.
- Refreshing a work's local files preserves indexed files in additional
  folders outside the scan depth or in offline storage pools.
- A local scan no longer takes a lyrics folder that **From remote** created
  inside a work folder, such as
  `Lyrics - CHI_HANS - RJ00000001 - 20261008-153012`, for a separate work.
  The next scan restores the work folder that holds such a folder.

</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>LLM, ASR, and content safety</strong></summary>

- Settings → **LLM & ASR** lists the configured LLM providers and ASR services
  above the content safety policy that governs both. Each service is added or
  edited in a dialog that lists the presets on the left and the chosen
  preset's form on the right. A service's switch turns it on or off from the
  list, and deleting one asks for confirmation.
- The tab stays behind an external service notice until an administrator
  accepts it: external services may forbid the content sent to them, the
  content safety checks are advisory, and Kikoto is not responsible for
  suspended accounts. The server refuses LLM and ASR configuration until then.
- LLM providers are built-in or custom OpenAI-compatible, Anthropic-compatible,
  Ollama, and Kikoto Helper endpoints. ASR services are OpenAI-compatible
  speech recognition services: OpenAI, a local deployment, or a custom
  service. A service on the server's own machine is reached through the host
  gateway, so only its port is entered.
- A provider or service offers several models from one connection, one of
  them the default. Translating and transcribing choose a provider and model,
  and a task keeps the model it started with. Model discovery for OpenAI
  speech recognition suggests only Whisper models.
- An LLM provider sets its request timeout, 10 minutes by default, so a local
  model has time to finish. An Ollama provider can also set its context length
  and turn off thinking, and Kikoto constrains Ollama's output to one entry per
  tag or lyrics line, so a small local model cannot drop or merge lines.
- **Kikoto Helper** is both an ASR service and an LLM provider preset. It
  reaches a Kikoto Helper on the server's machine on ports 17656 and 17657 by
  default, or on another machine by origin, with the API token the helper
  shows. Fetching models records the translation directions the helper's
  model supports: translation starts from the interface language when the
  model can translate into it, shows other languages as unavailable, and
  refuses an unsupported direction before queuing. A helper without an
  installed model, with a model that cannot start or ran out of memory, or
  with a risk notice that is not accepted names that cause.
- The content safety policy grades works as General, Sensitive, or High
  sensitivity. Each service declares the highest level it may receive,
  separately from its data boundary, and new services receive only General
  content. Tag rules are removable chips with per-tag actions and keyword
  rules are compact rows; either can mark high-sensitivity content or block
  every service, including for all-ages works. Prechecks show the matched
  rules and the chosen service's maximum.
- A default-off option rates LLM translations only by keywords in the complete
  selected source text; transcription always assesses the work's metadata.
- Proxy has an **LLM & ASR** scope for providers and services at another
  address, off by default; one on the server's own machine always connects
  directly. A translation or transcription request that a proxy drops after it
  was sent fails instead of moving to the next proxy, so the service never
  receives it twice.
- Proxy has a **Bypass proxy** list of hostnames, `*.domain` suffixes, IP
  addresses, and networks that every scope reaches directly. A network also
  matches a hostname that resolves into it. It starts with this machine, the
  container host, private networks, and `*.local`, `*.lan`, `*.internal`,
  and `*.home.arpa`, so a LAN source behind a configured proxy connects
  directly unless its entry is removed.

</details>
